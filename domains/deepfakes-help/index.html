The Story

It already happened.

In 2024, a finance employee in Hong Kong wired $25 million after a video call with deepfaked colleagues. In 2025, fake celebrity scams generated billions in losses. Detection companies, fact-checking initiatives and crisis-response playbooks are all racing to catch up — but the public still doesn't know where to turn. When it happens to them, people don't look for “synthetic media verification solutions”. They look for help.

Composition. The subject and the purpose sit on either side of the dot: deepfakes, and help dealing with them.

How the concept could work

  1. Choose the incident type: video, image, audio or impersonation.
  2. Preserve URLs, files, timestamps, screenshots and context.
  3. Submit material for technical triage or expert review.
  4. Receive reporting and takedown guidance.
  5. Escalate high-risk cases to legal, fraud or cybersecurity support.

A focused first launch

Publish clear routes for individuals and organizations, with evidence-preservation guidance and links to current platform reporting procedures.
