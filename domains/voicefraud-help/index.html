The Story

It took 35 seconds.

In 2019, criminals cloned a UK energy CEO's voice and stole $243,000 in three phone calls. Since then, voice cloning went from lab demo to free mobile app. Banks now list voice fraud among their fastest-growing attack vectors — and vishing attempts have multiplied across every sector. Companies are building response programs: detection, training, verification protocols. The market needs a destination. This is the name it would choose.

Market context — 2026

Why this domain

The opportunity

Composition. “Voice fraud” describes the incident channel in plain language; “.help” gives the name an immediate response purpose.

  • Phonetics. Two short, familiar words that work in customer warnings, training materials and incident calls.
  • Quiet advantage. It avoids jargon such as “vishing” when the person at risk simply needs to know where to verify a suspicious voice request.

The problem

Customers and employees receiving suspicious calls or voice notes need a trusted way to verify the request, preserve evidence, avoid acting under urgency and reach the correct fraud team quickly.

What someone could build

Illustrative workflow

How the concept could work

  1. Select the interaction: call, voicemail, voice note or executive request.
  2. Follow independent identity-verification steps before acting.
  3. Preserve the number, recording, timestamps and payment instructions.
  4. Classify the likely fraud pattern and receive guidance.
  5. Escalate the case to fraud operations, security or support.

A focused first launch

Introduce a suspicious-call guide and independently verifiable contact routes, with an explicit explanation of who operates the service.
